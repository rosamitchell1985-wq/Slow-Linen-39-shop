Wool the colour the sheep grew it.

Fully fashioned, which is a real difference and not a marketing word.

Most knitwear is cut out of a big knitted panel the way woven fabric is cut, then sewn together. It is fast and it wastes about a fifth of the yarn. It also leaves a cut edge inside the seam, which is where pilling and eventual holes tend to start.

Fully fashioned knitting shapes each panel on the machine, adding and dropping stitches to form the armhole and the shoulder. Nothing is cut, so nothing frays, and the shoulder seam lands where your shoulder actually is. It takes about three times as long per garment.

The undyed part matters too. Wool is scoured to remove lanolin and then, normally, dyed. We stop after the scour. The four shades we sell are the natural fleece colours sorted at the mill, which is why an oat knit bought this winter will differ a little from one bought two winters ago.

More on skipping the dye house

Care, honestly

  • Wash rarely. Wool is self-cleaning to a degree; airing overnight fixes most of it.
  • When you do wash, use a wool cycle at 86°F or hand wash cool with a wool detergent.
  • Never hang a wet knit. Roll it in a towel, press, then dry flat and reshape.
  • Store folded, not hung, and use cedar rather than mothballs.
