Accessibility statement

Effective August 27, 2026 Last reviewed August 27, 2026

This statement is a starting template and should be reviewed by an attorney before going live. The technical commitments below are ours regardless of what the legal review concludes.

Our commitment

We want everyone to be able to read about our clothes, work out what size they need and place an order without difficulty. Accessibility is treated as part of building a page, not as an audit we run afterwards.

Conformance target

We aim to meet Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.1 Level AA. We believe this site substantially conforms, with the known exceptions listed below. Substantially conforming means we have tested the guidelines that apply and have fixed what we found, not that we claim perfection.

What we have built in

  • Semantic structure. Every page uses real landmark elements: a banner header, a navigation region, a main region and a content-info footer. Headings run in order without skipping levels.
  • Skip to content. The first thing a keyboard user reaches on every page is a visible skip link that jumps past the navigation.
  • Keyboard operation. Every control, including the menu, the dropdowns, the filters, the accordions, the product gallery, the size pickers and the cookie banner, is reachable and operable with a keyboard alone. Escape closes the menu, the dropdowns and the cookie preference dialog.
  • Visible focus. Focus outlines are never removed. They are drawn in a sage tone that meets contrast requirements against every background we use.
  • Colour contrast. Body text is at least 4.5:1 against its background and large text at least 3:1. Our brand sage and clay are used as decorative fills only; text on or beside them uses deepened variants that pass. Nothing depends on colour alone to convey meaning.
  • Text alternatives. Every meaningful image has descriptive alternative text. Purely decorative images, such as the fabric texture strips, carry an empty alt attribute so screen readers skip them.
  • Forms. Every field has a visible label attached to it. Errors appear next to the field they belong to, in text as well as colour, and are announced through a live region. Required fields are marked in text, not only with an asterisk.
  • Status messages. Filter counts, form results and size selections update through polite live regions rather than moving focus unexpectedly.
  • Motion. Every animation on this site is disabled when your system requests reduced motion. Nothing moves, scales or fades under that setting. No content is hidden behind an animation, so the site is complete with JavaScript disabled.
  • Zoom and reflow. Text scales to 200 per cent without loss of content or function, and the layout reflows to a single column at 320 CSS pixels wide without horizontal scrolling.
  • Touch targets. Interactive controls are at least 44 by 44 CSS pixels with spacing between them.
  • No traps. There are no entry interstitials, no auto-playing audio or video, no content that flashes, and no popup that blocks the page on load. The cookie banner sits at the bottom and does not cover the content.
  • Tables. Measurement tables use proper header cells with scope attributes and a caption explaining what the numbers are, and scroll horizontally inside their own container rather than pushing the page sideways.

How we test

  • Keyboard-only navigation of every page and every interactive component before release.
  • Screen reader spot checks with VoiceOver on macOS and Safari, and with NVDA on Windows and Firefox.
  • Automated checks with axe DevTools and Lighthouse on every template.
  • Manual contrast checks on every text and background pairing in the design system, including text over photographs.
  • Layout testing at 360, 768, 1024, 1440 and 1920 pixels wide, and at 320 pixels with text scaled to 200 per cent.

Automated tools catch perhaps a third of real accessibility problems. The manual passes are where we find the rest.

Known issues we are still working on

We would rather list these than let you discover them.

  • Long measurement tables. The size guide tables scroll horizontally on narrow screens. This is accessible but tedious with a screen reader. We are working on a stacked presentation for small viewports.
  • Product gallery thumbnails. Changing the main image updates its alternative text but does not announce the change. We plan to add a live region announcement.
  • Filter results. The result count is announced, but focus stays on the filter button rather than moving to the results. Some screen reader users would prefer a focus move; others would not. We are testing both.
  • Third-party advertising. Where advertising is served by an external network, we cannot control the accessibility of the advertisement itself. We can and do control that the slot is labelled, is not adjacent to controls, and never appears above the main content.

Getting help ordering

If any part of this site prevents you from ordering, we will take the order for you by telephone or email, at the same prices, with no surcharge and no minimum. Call (828) 555-0142 between 9am and 5pm Eastern, Monday to Friday, or email access@slowlinenorganic.com.

We can also read measurements aloud, describe photographs in more detail, or send you the size guide as plain text.

Reporting a barrier

Tell us and we will fix it. Email access@slowlinenorganic.com with the page address, what you were trying to do, and what got in the way. Your browser, operating system and assistive technology help but are not required.

We acknowledge every report within two business days. We aim to resolve a barrier that blocks a purchase within ten business days, and to give you a dated plan for anything larger. If you are not satisfied with our response, you can escalate to Marguerite Okonkwo directly at hello@slowlinenorganic.com.

Technical specification

This site relies on HTML, CSS and JavaScript. It is built to work in current versions of Chrome, Edge, Firefox and Safari on desktop and mobile, with the two most recent major versions of each supported. Content remains readable and navigable with JavaScript disabled; only the filters, the gallery, the accordions and the cookie preference dialog require it, and the underlying content of each is present in the page markup.

Formal approval

This statement was prepared on August 27, 2026 following a self-evaluation against WCAG 2.1 Level AA. It has not yet been reviewed by an external auditor. We intend to commission an independent audit and will publish the result here when we do.
