Cookie policy

Effective August 27, 2026 Last updated August 27, 2026

This policy is a starting template and should be reviewed by an attorney before going live. It is written to sit alongside the privacy policy, which governs personal information generally.

What a cookie is

A cookie is a small text file that a website asks your browser to store. On the next request the browser sends it back, which is how a site remembers that the cart it is showing you is yours. Browser local storage does a similar job with a different mechanism and no automatic transmission. This policy covers both, and we use the word cookie for both because the distinction rarely matters to the reader.

Our position, stated plainly

Cookies that are strictly necessary to run the site are set as soon as you arrive, because without them the site cannot hold a cart or remember your consent choice. Everything else, including all analytics and all advertising, is off until you switch it on.

On your first visit a banner appears near the bottom of the screen. It does not cover the page content, it does not block you from reading or shopping, and it offers three equally weighted choices: accept all, reject non-essential, or manage preferences. Rejecting is one click, the same as accepting.

Your choice is recorded in browser local storage under the key slo-consent-v1 and is honoured on every page. If you clear your browser storage, the banner will ask again, because we no longer have your answer.

Changing your mind

Use the cookie preferences link in the footer of every page on this site. It opens the same preference panel as the banner and takes effect immediately. You can also delete cookies and site data through your browser settings, which resets everything.

Californians should read the Do Not Sell or Share My Personal Information section of the privacy policy, which explains why consenting to advertising cookies may count as "sharing" under state law and how to opt out.

What Google does with this, in Google's terms

  • Google, as a third-party vendor, uses cookies to serve advertisements on this site.
  • Google's use of advertising cookies enables it and its partners to serve advertisements to you based on your visit to this site and to other sites on the internet.
  • You may opt out of personalised advertising at Google Ads Settings.
  • Third-party vendors and advertising networks other than Google may also serve advertisements here. You can opt out of many of them at once at aboutads.info or the Network Advertising Initiative opt-out page.
  • Opting out of personalisation does not stop you seeing advertisements. It stops them being selected using your browsing history.

Advertising placements on this site

Advertising slots on this site are labelled Advertisement, are visually distinct from editorial and product content, never sit above the main content of a page, and never appear on legal pages or the error page. No advertising script loads at all unless you have allowed the advertising category.

Do Not Track and Global Privacy Control

We do not act on the browser Do Not Track header, because no agreed standard exists for what a site should do on receiving it. We do honour the Global Privacy Control signal and treat it as a valid opt-out of sale and sharing, which in practice means the advertising category stays off.

Managing cookies in your browser

Every major browser lets you view, block and delete cookies. The controls live under Settings, usually within a Privacy or Site data section. Blocking all cookies will break the cart on this site and on most others. Blocking third-party cookies only will not.

Changes to this policy

If we add a cookie or a vendor, the tables above are updated before the change goes live and the date at the top changes. If the change affects a non-essential category, your existing consent is reset and the banner asks again.
