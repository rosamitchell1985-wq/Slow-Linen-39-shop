The offcut problem, and what we do with it.

Cutting a shirt out of a roll of linen leaves gaps. Even with a tight marker, roughly twelve to fifteen per cent of a roll ends up as pieces too small for a garment panel. Most of the industry bales that and sells it as rag or sends it to landfill.

The napkin set exists because an 18 inch square fits neatly into the space between two sleeve heads. The tote handles come from the strip along the selvedge. The scarf is cut from full-width remnants at the end of a roll, which is why it is a square and not a rectangle.

This is not a zero waste claim and we will not make one. It takes our cutting waste from about fourteen per cent down to about six. The rest goes to a mill in Georgia that pulls it back into fibre for insulation.
