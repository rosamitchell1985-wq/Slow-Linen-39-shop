Outerwear

Six layers, none of them lined.

Why our jackets feel stiff for the first fortnight.

Most commercial outerwear gets its shape from fusible interlining, a layer of glued synthetic that gives a crisp front on day one. It also delaminates. After forty or fifty washes the glue lets go and the front bubbles, which is why an old jacket often looks worse than an old shirt.

We build shape into the pattern instead. That means a new hemp-linen jacket is genuinely stiff when it arrives. Wear it for two weeks and the canvas breaks at the elbow, the shoulder and the front edge, and it stops fighting you. Six months in it is the softest thing you own that still holds a line.

The trade is real and worth naming: you do the breaking-in, not a factory washing machine. If you want a jacket that feels finished on day one, ours will disappoint you for a fortnight.

The canvas, up close

Hemp linen, 11.5 oz

Hemp gives the tensile strength, flax gives the hand. Woven in Lithuania on a rapier loom, washed twice before cutting so the shrinkage happens before you own it.

Cotton canvas, 10 oz

GOTS organic cotton, garment washed. Softer out of the box than the hemp blend and lighter to wear, but it will show a knee mark and a pocket line sooner.

Seams and repair

Flat felled at the side and armhole, bar tacked at every pocket corner. If a seam fails inside five years of normal wear we repair or replace it. Email us with a photograph.
