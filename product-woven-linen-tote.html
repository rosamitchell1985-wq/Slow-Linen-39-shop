Woven Linen Tote

$88 USD. Flat $8 shipping, or free on orders over $150.

Two layers of flax linen canvas, stitched together and then boxed at the base so the bag stands up when you put it down. The handles are cut along the selvedge, which is the densest part of the roll, and stitched through both layers with a box-and-cross at each anchor point.

It exists because cutting shirts leaves gaps in the roll that are too small for a garment panel and too good to throw away. That is also why it comes in two colourways rather than a full palette: we make it from what the garment range leaves behind.

Colourway

One size, 16 inches wide by 15 inches high. Both colourways come from the same cloth; the olive is plant dyed in a small run and will lighten a little in its first year.

Fabric

  • Composition: 100% European flax linen canvas
  • Weight: 7 oz per layer, doubled to an effective 14 oz
  • Source: offcut from the shirting and outerwear runs, same rolls
  • Dye: oat is undyed. Olive is plant dyed with weld and iron
  • Trim: vegetable-tanned leather care tag, no plastic hardware anywhere

Fit and capacity

  • Dimensions: 16 in wide, 15 in high, 5 in boxed base
  • Handle drop: 24 in, clears a winter coat on the shoulder
  • Load: tested to 30 lb without seam distortion
  • Inside: one patch pocket, 7 in by 6 in, no lining
  • Packs to: roughly the size of a folded shirt when empty

Care

  • Wash: machine wash cold, gentle cycle, remove the leather tag first
  • Dry: hang dry by the handles. Do not tumble dry
  • Press: iron the handles flat while damp if they twist
  • Stains: treat before washing. Linen holds oil marks if they set
  • Repair: handle or seam failure inside five years is repaired free

Sizing and use

  • Size: one size, not adjustable
  • Fits: a 15 inch laptop flat, or a full week of produce
  • Model: Renske is 5'7" and carries it on the shoulder in the fourth image
  • Weight empty: 12 oz
  • Not suitable for: wet loads left standing. Linen wicks and will mark

Where the cloth for this bag comes from

Every garment is cut from a marker, a plan that arranges the pattern pieces on the roll to waste as little as possible. Even a good marker leaves gaps: the space between two sleeve heads, the wedge beside a curved front hem, the strip along the selvedge.

Across a year of shirting production that adds up to a few hundred yards of perfectly sound linen in awkward shapes. The tote panels are drawn to fit those shapes. The handles are the selvedge strip, which is a happy accident because it is also the strongest part of the roll.

This takes our cutting waste from roughly fourteen per cent to roughly six. We are not going to call that zero waste, because it is not. The remaining six per cent goes to a mill in Georgia that shreds it back to fibre for building insulation.

Straight answers

Linen has very low stretch, which is one reason we use it. Loaded to 30 lb the handles lengthen by under a quarter inch and return when unloaded. What does happen is that they twist. Iron them flat while damp.

Yes, slowly and fairly evenly. Weld and iron on linen lightens perhaps a tone over the first year of regular sun exposure and then holds. If you want a colour that stays put, take the undyed oat, which has nothing to lose.
