Oat Crew Knit

$165 USD. Free US shipping on this order.

A plain crew neck, knitted at 12 gauge in undyed lambswool about four hours from the workroom. It is fully fashioned, which means each panel is shaped on the knitting machine rather than cut out of a knitted sheet. Nothing inside the garment has a cut edge.

Oat is not a dye name. It is the natural colour of white-fleece lambswool after scouring, which is why one batch reads a shade warmer or cooler than another. We do not correct for it.

Fabric

  • Finish: scoured, not dyed and not bleached

Fit

  • Cut: true to size with slight ease through the body
  • Shoulder: fully fashioned; the seam sits on the shoulder point
  • Neck: 2 inch rib crew, wide enough to pull on without stretching
  • Sleeve: set-in, finished with a 3 inch rib cuff

Care

  • Wash: wool cycle at 86°F or hand wash cool, wool detergent only
  • Dry: roll in a towel, press out water, dry flat and reshape
  • Never: hang wet, tumble dry, or wring the sleeves
  • Store: folded, with cedar. Hanging stretches the shoulder permanently
  • Pilling: normal in the first month at the underarm. Use a comb, not a razor

What fully fashioned actually costs

A cut-and-sew knit takes roughly twenty minutes of machine time and wastes about a fifth of the yarn as offcut. A fully fashioned one takes closer to an hour and wastes almost nothing, because each panel comes off the machine at final shape. The yarn saving does not pay for the extra hour, which is why most of the industry stopped.

What you get for the difference is a garment with no cut edges inside it. Cut edges are where knitwear fails: they fray under the seam, the seam gives, and the shoulder line goes. A fully fashioned knit that is washed properly stays the same shape for a decade.

It also fits better on a real shoulder, because the armhole is knitted to a curve rather than cut to one. You notice this most when you raise your arms.
